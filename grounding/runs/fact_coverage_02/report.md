# Fact coverage 02: a test-design method for exposing fact failures

**Solver:** Qwen `qwen3.8:27b`. **Trials:** 3 per test. **Verdicts:** every exposing or unclear trial was read in
full before it counted. Tables are generated by [tables.py](tables.py) ([tables.md](tables.md)); verdicts are in
[manual_labels.json](manual_labels.json).

## Summary

- **The method finds many more distinct fact failures than cover-style tests.**
  - On facts the pilot never tested, 58 one-decoy probes exposed **13 distinct facts** (12 uncontested). The 18
    cover-style controls of the same scenarios exposed **2**.
  - On the pilot's own facts, the method's 125 probes exposed **25**. The pilot's 31 cover cases, rerun at 3
    trials, exposed **7**.
  - Probes found 32 facts that no cover test found. Cover tests found 3 that no single-decoy probe found, 2 once
    the fact probes of §12.2 count (`A:Task.message` was also exposed by a fact probe).
- **Per test:**
  - On the pilot's facts the two forms are about equally efficient: 0.20 facts per probe, 0.23 per cover case.
  - On new facts, probes are twice as efficient: 0.22 against 0.11.
  - The method's gain is breadth. Probes scale with the number of decoys; cover tests stop at one per scenario.
  - Cover tests catch another behaviour, but only when the layout hides the target: when a decoy is the only
    candidate on the path the request leads to, and the target sits one step off it, Qwen can settle for the decoy.
    In two of the three cases it read the mismatch and accepted the decoy anyway.
- **Repetition matters as much as design.** Trial 1 alone finds:
  - 17 of the method's 25 facts on the pilot's facts;
  - 8 of its 13 on new facts;
  - 4 of B1's 7.

  On the pilot's facts, the method's suite at one trial finds about what the pilot's 125 cases found (17 vs 16). The
  3 trials make the difference.
- **Every substitute family earns its tests.** 16–32% of each family's probes expose a failure (F3 has a single
  probe, which failed), and almost every fact is exposed by exactly one family. Plain decoys (F0) add 7 facts no
  other family found, so they cannot be dropped. Packing them into one test saves tests but loses facts.
- **The policy bug is near universal.** When a request presupposes a match that does not exist, Qwen acts on a near
  miss, and it resolves a singular request with two matches without asking. One test per domain shows this.
- **Three things the method needs.**
  - A validity pre-check, because two replica filters are silently ignored, one fact cannot be read, and two
    scenarios could not execute.
  - A check on written values, because Linear priorities were written on the wrong scale in 7 of 14 trials.
  - A cover control per scenario.
- **Follow-ups (§12).**
  - **Budget counted in runs.** On the recorded trials, a probe's first run finds about 4 times what a repeat finds.
    So under a run budget, run every probe once, then repeat probes, and run covers last.
  - **All of a fact's decoys in one probe.** For the 19 facts with several decoys, one probe holding all of a fact's
    decoys exposed 6 facts with 19 tests. One decoy per probe exposed 5 with 44 tests.
  - **Equal runs.** Given the same runs per fact (75 extra runs), the fact probes exposed 7 facts and the singles 5.
    One "alone only" fact turned out to be a run-count effect.
  - **Different failures.** Together, Qwen picks the "best match" among near misses; alone, it accepts a near miss
    outright. Two of the facts are new, making **43 distinct facts** in all.
- **Recommended procedure:** [§11](#11-recommended-method-v2).

## 1. Question

Design a procedure that, from a domain model, builds a suite exposing as many **distinct fact failures** as possible
with as few **test cases** as possible.

- **Budget** is the number of distinct test cases. Every test runs **3 trials**; trials measure how often a failure
  occurs and are not budget. Tokens are telemetry.
- **Substitutes** (what a decoy offers in place of the requested fact) must come from the domain model: its
  relationships and data types. Paraphrase or rewording of the request never counts as a substitute.
- **Validation is prospective:** the method is judged on facts and substitutes the pilot never tested, in all four
  domains (Box, Calendar, Linear, Slack).
- **Baselines** on the pilot's facts:
  - B1: the pilot's 31 cover cases, rerun at 3 trials;
  - B2: the pilot's 125 fact-sensitive cases, which exposed 16 bugs ([bugs.md](../fact_coverage_01/bugs.md)).
- **Solver:** Qwen (`qwen3.8:27b`) on the toy harness through Purdue GenAI. No automation is claimed: every scenario
  and every verdict is manual.

## 2. Method v1

[method.md](method.md) fixed the procedure before any v1 suite was generated or run, and its analysis plan before
any method-suite result came in.

- **Decoys.** A scenario is a natural request with one target. It has one decoy per (fact, substitute), and each decoy
  satisfies every condition but its fact. Every decoy claim is checked mechanically ([fdc.py](../fact_coverage_01/fdc.py)).
- **Substitute families**, from the domain model:
  - F1 sibling role or attribute;
  - F2 indirection;
  - F3 direction;
  - F4 hierarchy level;
  - F5 split;
  - F6 representation;
  - F7 neighbouring value by data type (the adjacent day, the next integer);
  - F8 partial identity;
  - F0 plain (no substitute).
- **Tests:**
  - **probe:** one decoy, no target, and "If there isn't one, just tell me.";
  - **packed plain test:** a scenario's F0 decoys together;
  - **target-present layer:** only for set answers;
  - **policy panel** of three tests per domain.

## 4. Scoring

[score.py](score.py) attributes every trial from its state diff with the pilot's analyzer. Acting on a decoy exposes
that decoy's fact. The script also flags three cases for review:
- a write command that names a decoy but changed nothing (for example, a request the service rejected);
- an answer that names a decoy without saying there is no match;
- an answer that is unclear.

Every exposing or flagged trial was read in full (trajectory, answer, diff) before it counted. Verdicts that differ
from the automatic label are in [manual_labels.json](manual_labels.json), with a note for each.

- A **replica artifact** (the replica, not the agent, caused the outcome) makes a trial not established.
- **Written values** are checked apart from grounding. A value that contradicts the request (Linear's priority scale)
  is reported as a value failure, not as a grounding bug.

### 5.2 Policy panel

The panel measures resolution behaviour, not facts:
- **P1:** the target removed, the request still presupposing a match;
- **P2:** one plain decoy, presupposing;
- **P3:** two records that both fully match a singular request.

| Domain | P1 (no target, presupposing) | P2 (one plain decoy, presupposing) | P3 (two full matches) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Box | BOX-01-A 3/3 | BOX-01-A-I12 3/3 | BOX-01-TWIN 3/3 |
| Calendar | CAL-06-A 3/3 | CAL-05-I14 0/3 | CAL-09-TWIN 3/3 |
| Linear | LIN-01-A 3/3 | LIN-01-A-I15 2/3 | LIN-15-TWIN 3/3 (changed both) |
| Slack | SLK-21-A 3/3 | SLK-21-A-I14 3/3 | SLK-23-TWIN 3/3 |

- **P1 and P3 fail in every domain in every trial.** With no match, Qwen acts on the nearest record. With two
  matches, it picks one silently, or in Linear changes both, and never asks.
- **P2 is the only variation.** In Calendar, a single plain decoy (a focus block at the Cafe instead of the Library)
  was reported rather than deleted.
- **One P1 and one P3 test per domain are enough** to establish the policy bug. P2 adds a measurement, not a bug.

## 9. Replica artifacts and invalid tests

A trial counts only when the agent, not the replica or the seed, decided the outcome.

**Filters the replica accepts but ignores.** These are the most dangerous for probes. With only one record left, an
ignored filter returns the decoy as if it matched, and an agent that trusts the server's filtering acts on it.
- **Calendar `events.list` ignores `eventTypes`.** A focus-time query also returns default-type events. In B1, Qwen
  deleted CAL-05-TOLD's decoy, a default-type event titled "Focus time", in 2 of 3 trials. In one of them Qwen sent
  the real API's `eventTypes=focusTime`. Both trials are not established.
- **Linear `issues(filter: {subscribers: …})` is ignored.** The schema accepts the filter, but `apply_issue_filter`
  has no `subscribers` branch. In P-LIN-26-I11 t3 and P-LIN-26-I13 t2, Qwen filtered on Dana's subscription and got
  back an issue she does not follow. Both trials are not established.

**A fact the replica does not expose.** The Linear project lead cannot be read.
- `projects` and `project(id)` return non-null errors, and `searchProjects` returns `lead: null` although the seed
  sets every project's lead.
- The LIN-03 probes turn on the project lead. P-LIN-03-I12 t1 updated the milestone of the project Sam leads and is
  not established. The other LIN-03 trials did not act on a decoy.
- Any pilot claim on `R:Project.leadId` needs to be read with this in mind.

**Scenarios that could not execute as first written.** Both were fixed, rebuilt and rerun at 3 trials in their
own directories. Their first-version trials are kept as evidence and not counted.
- **LIN-25 used label ids like `lab-reg`.** The replica's `issueUpdate` rejects label ids that are not UUIDs, as
  Linear does, so the control flailed and timed out in 3/3. The rerun uses UUID label ids:
  [runs/method_new_lin25](runs/method_new_lin25).
- **SLK-21 asked for `:white_check_mark:`.** The Slack replica accepts only about 50 listed reactions, and this one is
  not among them, so Qwen spent turns trying names. The rerun asks for `:thumbsup:`:
  [runs/method_new_slk21](runs/method_new_slk21).
  - The first version's grounding choices, taken before any rejected write, agree with the rerun. The day-before
    message was taken in P-SLK-21-I12 (2 of 3 trials) and in SLK-21-A (3 of 3).

**A pilot case the actor cannot complete.** CAL-07's actor has writer access to the target calendar, so the
description update returns 403. All three B1 trials identified the right calendar and count as correct grounding.

**Prevention for the method.** Before a probe runs, the author should issue the natural filter query for its fact
against the replica with the probe's seed, and confirm the decoy is excluded. Every write the scenario needs should
also be tried once against the prepared environment. Both checks are mechanical. With the existing observability
check, they would have caught all six problems above before any episode ran.

- **Replica or seed artifacts** (9 in total):
  - the ignored `eventTypes` filter: CAL-05-TOLD t1 and t3, P-CAL-05-I11 t1–t3;
  - the ignored `subscribers` filter: P-LIN-26-I11 t3, P-LIN-26-I13 t2, and one extra FP-LIN-26 run;
  - the unobservable project lead: P-LIN-03-I12 t1.
- **Other:**
  - LIN-02-TOLD t2 claimed an authentication error after one schema error and stopped;
  - P-LIN-07-I12 t2 and two FP-LIN-22 runs claimed Linear has no documents;
  - P-LIN-09-I13 t2 hit the 40-turn limit.
- **Timeouts.** None remain. With 6 concurrent episodes sharing the 19/min limit, 22 episodes (21 Linear, 1 Slack)
  timed out at 480 seconds. All 22 finished on retry at concurrency 3, one of them at the turn limit. Of the 3
  infrastructure errors, 2 completed on retry.

## 10. Written values

Grounding asks which record the agent acted on. It says nothing about the value written. For Linear's priority, the
value is where the failure shows most often. Linear's scale is 0 none, 1 urgent, 2 high, 3 medium, 4 low; the
API also exposes `priorityLabel`.
- **Writing.** 14 trials wrote a priority, in B1, the new-facts suite and the pilot-facts suite. **7 wrote the wrong
  value:**
  - Urgent written as 4 (Low) in all 3 trials of LIN-14;
  - High written as 3 in 2 trials;
  - Low written as 1 (Urgent) in 2 trials.
- **Reading.** The same confusion causes 5 grounding failures on `A:Issue.priority`:
  - LIN-01 in B1, trial 2;
  - P-LIN-01-I12 in trial 1 ("priority 4.0/Urgent");
  - the nearest-value probe PB-LIN-01-I12 in 2 of 3 trials;
  - the packed plain test PP-LIN-01 in trial 1.
- **Qwen never looked up the scale.** None of these trials read `priorityLabel` before writing. Which mapping Qwen
  used varies from trial to trial.

**Implication for the method.** Every state-changing test already writes a value. Checking that value against the
request costs no extra test and catches encoding facts: enums, scales, date and time representations. The scorer
does this for Linear priority ([score.py](score.py), `value_errors`). It should be generalized to each written
attribute that has an encoded value space.

## 11. Recommended method (v2)

Each step is marked **kept** from v1, **new**, or **changed**.

1. **Scenarios** (kept). Write a natural request whose identifying path combines several facts, with one target.
   Add one decoy per (fact, substitute), with substitutes drawn from the domain model by family (F1–F8), and a plain
   decoy (F0) for a fact with no substitute. Check every decoy claim mechanically.
2. **Validity pre-checks** (new). These are mechanical and run before any solver episode. Each would have caught one
   of this run's invalid tests.
   - **Write feasibility.** Perform the scenario's write on the target in a prepared environment. This catches id
     formats (LIN-25), value lists (SLK-21) and permissions (CAL-07).
   - **Filter fidelity.** For each fact, issue the replica's natural filter for it (`eventTypes`, `IssueFilter.subscribers`)
     against the probe's seed, and require that the decoy is excluded. If the replica ignores the filter, the probe
     measures the replica, not the agent.
   - **Observability** (kept). The fact's field appears in the natural read.
   - **Anchors survive** (new, now asserted by every suite builder; see [anchors.py](anchors.py)). In a test
     without a target, every other entity the request names must still exist: the group a label must belong to,
     the team, the creator, the channel. Only then is "there isn't one" true because the target is missing, and
     only then is a decoy a genuine near miss. For example, "the Regression label from the Bug group" needs the Bug
     group to exist, just without that label. Target removal deletes only the target and what points to it, never
     what it points to. The check makes this explicit: all 320 anchors in the 215 no-target cases are present.
3. **Tests per scenario.**
   - **One cover test** (changed: kept as a required test, not a control). It uses the request as written, with the
     target and all decoys. It catches agents that settle for the only near miss in view: 3 facts here that no
     single-decoy probe found, 2 once the fact probes count. All three came from layouts in which the target sat off
     the path the request leads to.
   - **One probe per decoy, F0 included** (kept). The decoy alone, no target, and "If there isn't one, just tell
     me." Families barely overlap, so each probe adds its own facts.
   - **For a fact with several decoys, one fact probe holding all of them** (new, from §12.2). It is the cheaper
     first test and catches best-match choices; add its single-decoy probes when the budget allows.
   - **Packed plain tests only under a hard budget** (changed). Here they found 3 of the 6 F0 facts with a quarter
     of the tests.
   - **The target-present layer** (kept), for requests whose answer is a set.
4. **Trials** (kept). Run 3 per test and report the failure frequency. One trial finds about two thirds of what
   three find. If runs rather than tests are the budget, run every probe once before repeating any, and run covers
   last (§12.1).
5. **Scoring** (changed).
   - Attribute from the state diff, and also scan the write commands. A write that names a decoy counts even when
     the service rejected it, including URL-encoded ids.
   - Read every exposing or unclear trial. For read-only questions, do not trust name matching: most "exposures" of
     LIN-06 were answers that named a decoy only to exclude it.
   - Mark replica or seed artifacts as not established, and flag contestable decoys.
   - Check written values against the request for every encoded attribute: enums, scales, dates (§10).
6. **Policy panel** (changed): one P1 test (target removed, presupposing) and one P3 test (two full matches) per
   domain. P2 adds nothing to the verdict.

**Expected cost and yield (Qwen).**
- A scenario with *k* decoys costs *k* + 1 tests.
- At 3 trials, about 0.2 distinct facts per test, on both the pilot's facts and new ones.
- If the budget forces cuts, drop the lowest-yield probes first: F0 (16%), then F7 (18%). Every family found facts
  no other found, so any cut loses facts.

**Caveats.**
- **One model.** The yields are Qwen's; Claude may fail on different families.
- **Few scenarios.** There are 4–6 new scenarios per domain, and all scenarios and verdicts are manual.
- **The forms differ in more than one way.** Cover test and probe differ in target presence, decoy count and wording
  at once, so the comparison is between forms as a whole, not a single variable.
- **One contestable decoy.** `A:File.name` rests on "Pricing sheet 2025.xlsx"; it is counted with an asterisk.
- **One unresolved question.** LIN-26 carried a realism caveat, recorded in advance: real Linear auto-subscribes
  assignees and creators. Both LIN-26 failures turned out to be replica artifacts, so the caveat never had to
  decide a verdict.

## 12. Follow-ups

### 12.1 Budget counted in runs

The main comparison counts a test as failed if any of its 3 runs fails. That favours arms with more tests: 125 probes
got 375 runs, and the 31 cover cases got 93. This follow-up asks a different question: if every run counted against
the budget, and a tester could repeat a test or move on to a new one, which allocation finds the most?

No new runs were made. The recorded trials were replayed in order (t1, t2, t3) under fixed run budgets, with the
code in [followups.py](followups.py).

| Use of a run (both fact sets pooled) | Runs | New facts | Per run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First run of a probe | 183 | 25 | 0.137 |
| Repeat of a probe (2nd or 3rd run) | 366 | 13 | 0.036 |
| Cover run, counting only facts the probes did not find | 147 | 3 | 0.020 |

| Policy | Pilot facts, 200 runs | New facts, 100 runs |
|---|---:|---:|
| Probes once each, then repeat probes | **21** | **11** |
| Probes once, covers once, then repeat probes | 20 | 10 |
| Covers ×3, then probes once | 19 | 9 |
| Probes once, covers once, then repeat covers | 18 | 9 |
| Covers only, ×3 (93 and 54 runs) | 7 | 2 |

- **A first run is equally productive in either form,** about 0.13 new facts per run for a cover and for a probe.
  Repeats are not.
- **A cover's first run mostly re-finds what probes find.** On the pilot's facts, all 4 facts from the covers' first
  runs were also found by a probe's first run. The covers' two unique facts appeared only on a third run.
- **Repeating covers has a ceiling.** From the facts seen once or twice in three runs, about 11 of the pilot's facts
  can ever be reached by repeating covers (7 observed), and about 2 on new facts.
- **So under a run budget:** run every probe once, highest-yield families first; then repeat probes; covers last.
  - Covers remain the main source of "settles for the only near miss in view" failures, but those were rare
    (3 facts in 147 runs, 2 once the fact probes count).
  - Under a budget of tests (§11), a cover is one cheap test per scenario and stays.
- **Caveats.**
  - Each policy is a single replay of the recorded trial order.
  - No test has more than 3 runs, so the covers' ceiling is an estimate.
  - The family order was measured on these same runs.

### 12.2 All of a fact's decoys in one probe

**Question.** For a fact with several decoys, is it better to probe the decoys one at a time (the method's probes)
or all together? The combined test is a **fact probe**: no target, every decoy of that one fact, the other facts'
decoys removed, and "If there isn't one, just tell me."

**Tests.** There are 19 fact probes, run at 3 trials each (57 runs), in [suite_factprobe.py](suite_factprobe.py) and
[runs/factprobe](runs/factprobe). They cover every fact with 2 or more decoys:
- **15 new facts** (36 decoys);
- **4 pilot facts**, each pairing its plain decoy with the nearest-value decoy. That decoy had been the same record
  at a second value, so it is added as a clone with fresh ids and a distinct name.

Each is compared with the same decoys' one-at-a-time probes (44 tests, 132 runs), which already ran. Every exposing
or unclear trial was read; the verdicts are in [manual_labels.json](manual_labels.json).

| | Tests | Runs | Facts exposed | Per test |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All of a fact's decoys together | 19 | 57 | **6** | 0.32 |
| One decoy per probe | 44 | 132 | **5** | 0.11 |
| Both | 63 | 189 | 8 | 0.13 |

- **Exposed only together (3):**
  - SLK-22 thread (3/3);
  - SLK-23 channel purpose (2/3);
  - BOX-24 task message (2/3).

  Every single-decoy probe for these three facts passed 0/3.
- **Exposed only alone (2):**
  - CAL-24 calendar location: the name decoy failed 3/3 alone and 0/3 together;
  - LIN-25 label group: 2/3 alone, 0/3 together.
- **Exposed both ways (3):**
  - CAL-21 event description;
  - LIN-01 priority (3/3 together, taking the Medium clone every time);
  - LIN-23 resolver.
- **New overall:** the SLK-22 thread and SLK-23 purpose facts are exposed by no other test in this study. That
  brings the total to **43 distinct facts** (42 uncontested) and the new-facts total to 16. BOX-24's task message
  had already been exposed by its cover control.
- **Comparing instead of checking.** Where the combined probe failed, Qwen chose the closest near miss instead of
  concluding "none". Its answers say so:
  - SLK-23: "the best match for coordinating vendor contracts";
  - SLK-22: Diego's reply, "in a thread about a search latency spike, where he referenced the checkout outage".

  Beside a clearly wrong decoy (a top-level post, which is not a reply at all), the other decoy looks like the
  answer by elimination.
- **Checking instead of comparing.** In CAL-24 and LIN-25, seeing four "Tokyo" calendars or two "Regression" labels
  side by side made Qwen check the distinguishing field (location, label group) and report none.
- **Grouping decoys cuts both ways.** Packing decoys of *different* facts together suppressed failures: 4/84 against
  17/93 alone (§5.1), as in the pilot. Grouping decoys of the *same* fact brings out a different failure, the
  best-match choice, while hiding some that appear alone.

**Checking for a run-count effect.** At 3 trials each, a fact probe had 3 runs, while its single-decoy probes had
3 per decoy (6 to 12). Could the facts found only alone just reflect the extra runs?

The 75 extra runs in [runs/factprobe_extra](runs/factprobe_extra) gave each fact probe 3 runs per decoy, the same as
its singles. Every exposing trial was read. The comparison below is per fact, at equal runs, with a one-sided Fisher
test. Facts that failed in neither form are omitted; the full table is in [tables.md](tables.md).

| Fact | Together: failing runs | Alone: failing runs | p (difference by chance) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SLK-22 reply's thread (`H:Message.parent_id`) | 5/6 | 0/6 | 0.008 |
| SLK-23 channel purpose (`A:Channel.purpose_text`) | 3/6 | 0/6 | 0.09 |
| BOX-24 task message (`A:Task.message`) | 2/6 | 0/6 | 0.23 |
| CAL-21 event description | 2/6 | 3/6 | 0.50 (alone more) |
| LIN-23 comment resolver | 4/6 | 3/6 | 0.50 |
| LIN-01 priority | 4/6 | 3/6 | 0.50 |
| CAL-24 calendar location (`A:Calendar.location`) | 1/12 | 3/12 | 0.30 (alone more) |
| LIN-25 label group (`H:IssueLabel.parentId`) | 0/6 | 2/6 | 0.23 (alone more) |
| **All 19 facts, pooled** | **21/129 (16%)** | **14/130 (11%)** | |

- **CAL-24 was partly a run-count effect.** With equal runs, the fact probe also exposed it (once in 12). Its "only
  alone" status came mostly from having 3 runs against 12.
- **LIN-25 is the only fact still found only alone,** and the difference is within chance (p = 0.23).
- **The together-only facts hold up.** SLK-22 fails 5/6 together and 0/6 alone. SLK-23 and BOX-24 point the same
  way, weakly.
- **At equal runs,** together exposes **7 facts** and alone **5**. The failure rate per run is 16% against 11%.
- **Caveat on the statistics.** There are 19 comparisons with 6–12 runs each. Only SLK-22 is significant on its own,
  and not after correcting for 19 comparisons. The pattern is consistent, but individual facts are not settled.

**Implication for the method.** A fact with several decoys should get a fact probe first.
- At equal runs it exposed more facts than the one-decoy probes (7 against 5), and it needs 19 tests where the
  singles need 44.
- Its specific extra failure is the best-match choice among near misses, which one decoy at a time cannot produce.
- The singles are still worth running when the budget allows: LIN-25 may be a real "alone only" case, and the
  singles show which decoy a failing fact turns on.
