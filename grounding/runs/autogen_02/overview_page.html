Near-miss tests for AI agents: the automated system and what it found

An agent working in Box, Google Calendar, Linear or Slack has to act on exactly the record the user means. This system writes tests that tempt it with records that are almost right, runs them against an agent (Qwen 3.8, 27B) in replicas of the four services, and grades the results.

The kinds of test

Every test is a request and a seeded world. The world holds the target, the record the request means, and near misses: records that match every condition but one. Each near miss fails on one fact, such as the organizer, the day or the title.

TestThe requestRight behaviour
Coverfits exactly one record, among near missesact on the target only
Probetarget removed, one near miss left, plus “If there isn’t one, just tell me”say there is none
Fact probetarget removed, all near misses of one fact left, plus the same escape clausesay there is none
Absence twin policya probe without the escape clause: the request presumes the record existssay there is none
Underspecified policytwo or more records match fully: one condition dropped (“drop-F”) or the target copied (“clone”)ask which one, or report the matches, before changing anything

The first three are regular tests, one per fact. The policy tests ask whether the agent fails depending on the fact, or whatever the fact. If it fails whatever the fact, one policy test per service is enough.

  • The rule, fixed before any run: a cell is policy-level when we are 90% confident that a test on a random fact fails more than 80% of the time. 11 failures out of 11 is the least that shows it.
  • Three decisions each counted one test later found flawed or contested. A robustness run declared in advance confirmed two of them, and a ruling settled the third: the Slack bot counts as a channel member, as Slack’s own API counts it.
  • The new scenarios’ policy tests agree: 88 of 90 absence trials and 106 of 108 underspecified trials fail.

A fact is exposed when the agent fails at least one trial of a test that turns on it. The 29 new scenarios ran in two batches of different scenarios, not a rerun. Their facts barely overlap (one fact is in both), so 19 and 5 are different facts. The suites test different facts, so comparisons between them are rough, and difficulty varies widely between scenarios from the same process.

What the agent does

  • It almost never asks. Asking is not required: reporting the matches without acting also counts as right. Even so, it happened once in 435 underspecified trials. Given several matches, it acts on the first it finds (93 of 129) or on all of them (30), and acting first then disclosing still counts as a failure.
  • It acts when the request presumes a record. About 95% of absence trials act on a near miss. For 14 of 30 new facts it passes the probe and fails the twin.
  • It often sees the mismatch and acts anyway. Of 169 absence failures graded by hand, it misread the deciding field in 37%, noted the mismatch and acted anyway in 34% (“likely a typo”, “the closest match”, “clearly the one you meant”), and never checked in 22%.
  • It changes the world to fit the request. It unarchived a channel so that it could invite someone to “the channel that hasn’t been archived”, created a missing team, and posted the comment the request described as already there.
  • Its one reliable check is who posted a Slack message and where: those absence tests pass 9 of 9.

The judge

On random trials a person graded before seeing its verdicts, the LLM judge agrees in 87 of 93 on the first policy runs, then in 110 of 110. Three of the six early disagreements were later ruled in the judge’s favour. When it says “fail” it is right 88 of 89 times, and it finds every failure the person found. It cannot tell a flawed test from a failing agent.

What these results do not cover

  • One agent. Everything here is Qwen 3.8 (27B); another agent may fail differently.
  • Three kinds of test left out: requests meant for several records, requests the service cannot do, and attributing why a failure happened.
  • 42 facts wait on replica fixes, mostly Linear projects. 11 replica issues are documented, 4 found in this study. Another 54 are drawn into briefs but not yet generated.
  • Scenario difficulty varies widely, so one batch is not enough to rate a writer.
