# Automated test generation and evaluation with Claude Code Sonnet agents

**Question.** Can Claude Code Sonnet agents, inside fixed scaffolding, generate fact-discrimination tests and judge an
agent's trials at the standard of the hand-built exemplars? The exemplars are fact_coverage_01's pilot and
fact_coverage_02's suites, with their 267 manual labels.

**Setup.**
- **Agents:** Sonnet (`claude-sonnet-5`) in headless Claude Code, on the user's subscription.
- **Solver:** Qwen `qwen3.8:27b` via Purdue, 3 trials per test.
- **Plan:** fixed before the runs in [plan.md](plan.md), with four dated amendments.
- **Runs:** the evening of 2026-09-25 to midday on 2026-09-26.
- **Tables:** [tables.md](tables.md), generated by [kit/tables.py](kit/tables.py). Numbers below come from there
  unless another script is named.

## Summary

**Not yet.** The generated tests are about as valid as the hand-built ones. On the same facts, though, they expose
fewer, and one round of iteration did not close that gap. The judge agrees with the manual labels on every exposed
fact, but misses its outcome bar.

| Pre-registered bar ([plan.md](plan.md)) | Result | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Judge: outcome agreement at least 90% on the held-out split | 142/177 (80%). On the trials of tests the manual review did not invalidate as whole tests, 139/141 (98.6%) | No |
| Judge: exposed-fact agreement at least 90% | 64/64 | Yes |
| Generation: manual validity at least the exemplars' | Arm R: 16/18 scenarios and 64/67 decoys valid. Arm P: 11/15 and 48/56. Arm P with method v2: 15/16 and 60/62. Exemplars: 1 contestable decoy of 58; of 21 scenarios, 2 fixed after their first run and 1 invalid test | Arm R: about equal. Arm P: no. v2: yes |
| Generation: yield within the exemplars' range (about 0.2 facts per test at 3 trials) | Arm R, on the exemplars' own facts: 0.11 per test (9 facts, 85 tests). Exemplars: 0.16 run the same day (12 facts, 76 tests); 0.18 recorded (14 facts, 13 uncontested) | No |

**What the runs show.**
- **Validity is where the automation holds up.** Before any solver run, code checks, replica pre-checks and a cold
  reader sent back 50 versions. Examples: decoys that failed two conditions, values the solver could not see,
  writes that did not land. The defects the exemplars needed fixing after running are now caught before a run: a
  reaction the replica rejects, non-UUID label ids, ambiguous wording.
- **What remains is what no check knows:**
  - domain semantics (a Linear label group applied as a label);
  - fields the actor may not read;
  - contrived wording.
- **Yield is the shortfall.** On the 36 Arm R facts, the generated suites exposed 9 facts and the exemplars 12, run
  the same day. That is 75% of the facts, or 69% per test, because the generated suites have more tests. The
  generated suites also exposed 3 facts the exemplars never did.
- **The yield gap is within the variation of a single run.** The exemplars themselves exposed 13 uncontested facts
  on their recorded runs and 12 today, with only 10 in common. With 36 facts and 3 trials, a 3-fact gap cannot be
  told from chance. It points the same way on every view (per test, per fact, per family), so I read it as "somewhat
  weaker, not shown to be weaker".
- **One iteration did not help yield.** Method v2 ("make the near miss tempting", drawn from the Arm R autopsy)
  regenerated the Arm P briefs. It exposed 11 facts against v1's 13 (0.12 against 0.15 per test). Its scenarios were
  valid more often (15/16 against 11/15), partly thanks to kit fixes made at the same time. Its rule to keep
  requests short was not followed: the requests got longer.
- **The judge is only as good as the replica profile.** I read 167 failing or void verdicts on the generated runs
  and overrode 8. Seven turn a failure into an artifact, and all seven are caused by the replica: an ignored filter,
  or a field the actor cannot read. Six of those trials rest on behaviour that the profile the judge reads does not
  describe, and the judge caught none of them.
- **The runs found three replica gaps the profiles did not list** (§7). They also showed that several nested Linear
  connections fail. That limits what Linear tests can establish, for the exemplars and the generated suites alike.
- **Cost.** Every Sonnet call of the study (development, calibration and main runs) comes to $222 at list price,
  billed to the subscription.
  - Per accepted scenario: $1.78 (Arm R) and $3.07 (v2). Arm P's $5.47 includes 11 briefs regenerated after the
    session limit.
  - Judging: $0.02 to $0.03 per trial.

## 1. What is automated, and what is not

| Part | Who | Counts as |
|---|---|---|
| Writing each scenario, and repairing it from findings | Sonnet writer ([kit/prompts/writer.md](kit/prompts/writer.md)) | automated |
| Reading each scenario cold (matches, the condition each decoy fails, ambiguity, naturalness) | Sonnet reader ([kit/prompts/reader.md](kit/prompts/reader.md)) | automated |
| Judging each trial that is not mechanically clean | Sonnet judge ([kit/prompts/judge.md](kit/prompts/judge.md)) | automated |
| Seed operations, mechanical checks, replica pre-checks, suite derivation, solver runs, triage, scoring | code ([kit/](kit/README.md)) | scaffolding, written by hand |
| The four domain additions, the seed vocabulary, two worked examples | my drafts ([inputs/](inputs/)) | manual domain input |
| Validity review of accepted scenarios; reading every failing or void verdict | me ([eval/validity.json](eval/validity.json), [eval/judge_review.json](eval/judge_review.json)) | manual evaluation |

**Isolation.** Every agent is a `claude -p` session:
- in restricted mode, with no MCP servers;
- working in a workspace under `/tmp`, outside any repository;
- the writer has Read, Write, Edit, Glob and Grep inside the workspace only; the reader and judge have no tools.

So no agent sees project instructions, memory, the manual labels, or the exemplar scenarios of the facts it is
assigned. Agents never certify their own work. Code runs every check, and every finding goes back to the same writer
session.

**Two disclosures.**
- The manual evaluation here (the validity verdicts and the reading of failing verdicts) is mine. I also built the
  kit and wrote the documents the writers read, so it is not independent. Both files are there for spot checks, the
  way the pilot's 16 bugs were reviewed.
- The four domain additions (`inputs/`) are manual input, not automation. The replica profile was drafted from
  fact_coverage_02's report §9, that is, from the manual review of the very runs that form the judge's test split.

## 2. The judge

**Setup.**
- **Input:** one trial at a time, the same evidence a manual reviewer reads ([kit/bundle.py](kit/bundle.py)):
  - the request;
  - the target and each decoy, with its fact and the author's explanation;
  - every step (reasoning, command, response);
  - the final answer;
  - the state diff;
  - the mechanical attribution.
- **Output:** a structured verdict ([kit/judge.py](kit/judge.py)): outcome, exposed facts, mechanism, and a note.
- **Split:** the 267 manual labels were divided by run before any change to the judge. Dev is the pilot-fact runs
  (130 labels, plus 20 clean trials); test is every other run (137 labels, plus 40 clean trials).

| Split | Trials | Outcome agreement | Exposed-fact agreement | Artifacts caught |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dev, round 1 | 150 | 128 (85%) | 63/78 | 1/6 |
| Dev, round 2 (frozen) | 150 | 144 (96%) | 87/87 | 4/6 |
| **Test (held out, run once)** | **177** | **142 (80%)** | **64/64** | **6/39** |

- **The pre-registered bar of 90% is missed on the test split.**
- **33 of the 35 misses are trials of three tests invalidated as whole tests.** The manual review invalidated SLK-21's
  first version (a reaction the replica rejects), LIN-25's first version (non-UUID label ids) and BOX-31 (ambiguous
  wording) as whole tests. The judge scores grounding trial by trial, so it cannot make those test-level calls.
- **On the other 141 trials it agrees on 139 (98.6%).** Both remaining disagreements are judgment calls (a rejected
  write on a decoy followed by a correct "none"; a correct choice never written).
- **In the automated pipeline the three defects are caught before any run:** the replica rules reject the reaction
  and the label ids, and the reader blocks BOX-31 (§3).
- **Where the judge's rules came from.** Between the dev rounds, five method rules that version 1 left implicit were
  written into its prompt, and the policy-panel mapping moved into code (plan.md amendment 1). Nothing in the
  prompt refers to a particular test.

**On the generated runs** there are no manual labels, so I read every failing, void or unclear verdict, as the
manual process did ([eval/judge_review.json](eval/judge_review.json)). That is 167 verdicts: Arm R 37, the control 44,
Arm P 52, v2 34.
- **I overrode 8.** Seven turn a failure into an artifact, all caused by the replica:
  - an ignored filter the replica profile lists (`subscribers`), which the judge knew and did not apply (1 trial);
  - filters the profile did not list: Slack's `types` (2 trials) and Linear's document project name (1 trial);
  - a field the actor cannot read: a calendar's sharing rules return 403 to a writer (3 trials).

  The eighth only reclassifies a void trial as an artifact (the broken attachments path, §7).
- **The judge caught artifacts only where the evidence or the profile named the cause.** It labelled 4 trials as
  artifacts itself: 3 on the listed `subscribers` gap, and 1 where the response showed the flag the seed had set
  differently. It caught none of the 6 trials that rest on replica behaviour the profile does not describe.
- **On the sampled clean trials** it agreed with the mechanical label on 54 of 56 in Arm R, 36 of 36 in the control,
  37 of 39 in Arm P and 46 of 48 in v2. All 6 disagreements are "not established": Qwen answered "none" after wrongly
  concluding that Linear has no documents (5), or after the broken attachments path (1). The manual labels treated
  this behaviour the same way.

## 3. The cold reader, calibrated on the exemplars

The 18 new scenarios and the 4 hidden-target tests of fact_coverage_02 were read with the reader's own conditions
(they have no author condition list) ([runs/reader_calibration_01](runs/reader_calibration_01)):
- **18 of the 20 valid exemplars pass.**
- **The known-invalid test is blocked.** H-BOX-31 fails on "about travel costs", the defect the manual review found.
- **The known-contestable decoy is flagged.** BOX-22's "Pricing sheet 2025.xlsx" is marked contestable.
- **Two valid exemplars are wrongly blocked.** BOX-23 ("contract PDF") and CAL-24 ("located in Tokyo", which is
  arguably debatable).

The first reader protocol also blocked intended near misses as "ambiguous". Writers then dropped their best F1 decoys
or added test-only hints (dev round 1). Protocol v2 separates a genuine ambiguity (a careful reader cannot tell which
meaning was intended) from a contestable decoy (a careful reader could argue it matches). A contestable decoy is kept
and flagged on the decoy, like the exemplars' asterisk.

**What each stage caught** ([kit/show_generation.py](kit/show_generation.py) prints every finding):
- **The checks (code):**
  - decoys that do not fail their condition when fdc checks the claim;
  - seed operations with missing arguments;
  - derived tests whose anchors vanish with the target, or that leave orphaned thread replies.

  After the post-run fix, they also re-check every derived test. That caught AP2-CAL-01's first version before any
  run: with the target removed, another calendar matched its query, so its probes would have had an answer.
- **The replica pre-checks (code):**
  - seeds the replica refuses (a duplicate team key, a comment on a missing document);
  - reads that fail;
  - decoy values no read returns;

- **The reader (agent):**
  - decoys that fail two conditions;
  - near misses the writer did not declare;

- the reader kept finding decoys that also failed the key condition;

## 6. Reproduction, and the one iteration

### Arm R, fact by fact

Of the 36 facts ([tables.md](tables.md), "Arm R, fact by fact"):
- **The exemplars exposed 14 on their recorded runs, 13 uncontested.** Today they exposed 12.
- **The generated suites exposed 8,** plus `A:User.name`, which is not a brief fact.
- **4 were exposed by both** the generated suites and today's exemplars: `R:HubItem.file`, `A:Task.created_at`,
  `R:Comment.resolvingUserId`, `A:Conversation.purpose_text`.
- **4 were exposed only by the generated suites.** 3 of these the exemplars never exposed: `A:Calendar.description`
  (CAL-22), `R:Issue.creatorId` (LIN-21) and `A:Cycle.number` (LIN-24). The fourth, `A:Event.description`, the
  exemplars exposed on their recorded runs only.
- **8 were exposed only by today's exemplars:** `A:Task.message`, `A:User.login`, `A:EventAttendee.email`,
  `A:Calendar.location`, `A:Issue.createdAt`, `H:IssueLabel.parentId`, `A:Message.created_at`, `H:messages.parent_id`.

**Why the generated decoys expose less** ([eval/autopsy.md](eval/autopsy.md)).
- **The exemplars' decoys that failed Qwen put the requested value where the agent looks first:**
  - a calendar *named* "Tokyo" (3/3);
  - a title that mentions the topic (3/3);
  - a login that contains the requested one, `pat.kimura` for `pat.kim` (3/3).
- **The generated decoys on the same facts put the value in less-read fields** more often: a location line, a
  description or a comment.
- **Their partial identities ran the other way,** such as `dana.white` for `dana.whitfield`, which a search for the
  requested value does not return.
- **The generated requests are also longer.**

### Method v2 on Arm P (plan.md amendment 4)

**What changed.**
- **The writer's method notes** gained "Make the near miss tempting":
  - put the substitute in a name, title or login;
  - make a partial identity contain the requested value;
  - keep the request as short as a user would write it.

  Its examples are the exemplars' own decoys (CAL-24's Tokyo calendar, BOX-24's `pat.kimura`), which is why v2 was
  tested on Arm P only.
- **The kit fixes of §7 applied too:**
  - the reader sees full records;
  - derived tests are re-checked;
  - replacement values get an observability check.

**Result, on the same briefs** ([tables.md](tables.md), "Arm P: method v1 against v2").
- **Facts exposed:** 13 in v1 (of 42 testable brief facts, since AP-LIN-03 was rejected) and 11 in v2 (of 45). 7 are
  common to both, 6 are v1's alone, and 4 are v2's alone. Two generations of the same briefs thus share 7 of the 17
  facts they expose between them.
- **Per test:** 0.18 against 0.12 on covers and probes, and 0.15 against 0.12 on all tests.
- **Validity improved:** 15 of 16 scenarios valid against 11 of 15, and 60 of 62 decoys against 48 of 56. The kit
  fixes probably account for part of this.

**Why yield did not improve.**
- **The requests got longer, not shorter:** 134 characters median against 127. AP2-SLK-01's request has 13
  conditions by the request-size count, the most of any scenario.
- **The families shifted, and not all to good effect.**
  - F8 grew from 8 probes to 13 and kept its rate (3 of 8; 5 of 13).
  - F7 went from 4 of 12 to 0 of 11, and F1 from 4 of 15 to 2 of 14.
- **One post-run ruling of mine counts against v2.** I judged AP2-SLK-01's display-name decoy contestable after
  seeing v2's failures. That removed v2's only exposing cover and the fact `A:User.display_name`. Without the ruling,
  v2 has 12 facts against v1's 13.

## 7. Shortcomings and problems

**In the generated scenarios** (my review, [eval/validity.json](eval/validity.json)):
- **Valid but less tempting decoys.** This is the main shortfall, and it is about quality, not validity (§6). Method
  v2 did not fix it.
- **A domain-semantics error no check caught.** AR-LIN-25 tags an issue with a label *group* itself, which Linear
  does not allow. Its only decoy carries a label from that group, and that is exactly what "tagged Customer Tier"
  naturally means. The reader only marked the decoy contestable.
- **A field the actor cannot read.** AP-CAL-02's third decoy differs only in a calendar's sharing rules, which a
  writer cannot list (403). The observability check passed, because the values appear in other responses. It checks
  that a value is reachable somewhere, not that the actor can reach it through the path it needs.
- **A structural error the checks missed.** AP-SLK-05 asks for "the most recently created channel …". Removing the
  target promotes an older candidate to the answer, so one probe has a correct answer while being scored as a
  failure. The checks ran fdc on the cover only. The kit now re-checks every derived test. In v2 that caught
  AP2-CAL-01's first version before any run, where another calendar matched once the target was removed.
- **Contrived or awkward requests.** AP-SLK-01 and AP2-SLK-01 duplicate names (two Farhan Maliks, two Priya
  Sharmas). AP-CAL-02 says "the Team Calendar that I have writer access to on my calendar list". The reader judged
  all three natural.
- **Family labels** are sometimes not the ones I would choose:
  - a sub-team labelled F2 rather than F4;
  - a subscriber-not-creator decoy labelled F2 rather than F1;
  - a due date for a created date labelled F6 rather than F1.

  This does not affect validity. It affects the per-family tables.
- **A few near-duplicate records** (four calendars named "Front Desk", four issues titled "Design system audit") are
  valid but less realistic than the exemplars' distinct names.

**In my review: reading the failing verdicts was a second validity pass.** It found two decoy problems the scenario
review had missed:
- AP-CAL-02's unreadable field;
- AP2-SLK-01's contestable display name, ruled after seeing v2's failures.

The automated loop has no equivalent, because the judge scores trials, not decoys.

**In the kit**, found by the runs and fixed afterwards. The fixes did not change what the solver saw; the reader's
fix changes what later readers see:
- **The reader's record rendering was lossy.** It dropped `url` fields and cut rows at 900 characters. That hid
  `Attachment.url` in AP-LIN-06 and a Linear team's `private` flag in AP-LIN-03, whose rejection this contributed to.
- **The observability check covered only decoys defined by a dropped or substituted condition.** For AR-SLK-23, the
  replica reports a group DM as private (as Slack does), so the "not private" decoy was invalid, and nothing flagged
  it. A text search cannot verify flags, so this needs a per-record check through the actor's own reads.
- **The effect locator's key** defaulted to `id` in the scorer, while the pre-check used the table's key. AR-SLK-23
  stopped the scoring step until it was fixed.
- **The derivation left orphaned rows** when the removed target was a Slack user. It was found by the end-to-end dev
  run and fixed before the main runs.
- **The judge's bundle is lossy too** (`bundle.compact_row` truncates rows and drops URLs). It was left unchanged,
  because the judge was frozen.

**In the replica: three gaps the profiles did not list, found by the runs.** I verified each in the replica code:
- **Slack's `users.conversations` ignores `types`.** A solver asking for private channels gets public ones too. One
  Arm R trial and one control trial acted on the public decoy that came back.
- **Linear's `documents(filter: {project: …})` honours only `project.id`.** A filter on the project's name is
  ignored. One control trial took the unfiltered result as a match.
- **Linear's `Issue.attachments` has no resolver.** Asking for an issue's attachments fails ("Cannot return null for
  non-nullable field AttachmentConnection.nodes"); only the top-level `attachments` query works.
  - In AP2-LIN-06, 11 of 24 trials never saw an attachment record; in AP-LIN-06, 3 of 12
    ([kit/check_attachments.py](kit/check_attachments.py)).
  - Those that answered "none" are scored as passes, though nothing was established.

**Nested Linear connections fail more widely.** This affects the exemplars and the generated suites alike
([kit/check_nested.py](kit/check_nested.py)). Besides `Issue.attachments`, these also fail:
- `Team.cycles` (LIN-24, AP-LIN-04);
- `Team.projects`;
- `Comment.children` (LIN-23, AP2-LIN-05);
- the top-level `projects` query: all 80 responses to it were errors (LIN-22, LIN-24, AP2-LIN-01, AP2-LIN-07).

The profile said only that `projects` fails. Solvers usually recover through a top-level query. When they don't, a
probe's "none" means nothing, and a failure the test was built to find cannot happen.

The ignored filters are artifacts under the method's rule, and I overrode them alike in every arm. They join the
ignored `eventTypes`, `subscribers` and `parent` filters in the list of replica paths a probe must not rely on.

**In the judge:**
- **It recognizes a replica artifact only when the profile or the evidence names the cause** (§2). With an
  incomplete profile, it reports unlisted gaps as the solver's failures.
- **Per-trial verdicts cannot express test-level decisions** (a superseded first version, an invalid test). That
  cost it the held-out bar (§2).

**In the operation:**
- **The session limit, twice.** The Claude Code session limit (HTTP 429) stopped 13 briefs at about 00:40. They
  waited for the reset and were regenerated from scratch at about 07:15. In the late morning it refused 32 of Arm R's
  judge calls and all 86 of v2's; those were rerun after the 12:10 reset. Verdicts are cached per attempt, so the
  rerun judged only the refused trials.
- **Evidence and telemetry.** Failed calls are kept, and their partial usage is counted.
- **Large files.** Seven transcripts over 1 MB (8.6 MB in all) are committed as ordinary blobs, as in the other
  grounding studies, not through Git LFS.

## 8. Manual decisions and interventions

All are recorded in the plan's amendments and in git.

**Before any main brief ran (setup and dev):**
- **Inputs, drafted by hand** (`inputs/`):
  - facts with substitute menus (derived by rule from the catalog);
  - replica profiles (from fact_coverage_02 §9 and the replica code);
  - seed vocabularies;
  - the solver's own API docs, extracted verbatim.
- **Two worked examples:** the pilot's BOX-01 and CAL-01, rewritten in the new format. The self-test shows that their
  seeds expand to exactly the pilot's rows.
- **Judge, round 2.** Five method rules the first version left implicit were written into the judge's prompt:
  - policy-panel scoring;
  - ignored filters are artifacts;
  - a rejected write on the target is correct grounding;
  - a clarifying question is a correct "none";
  - a record that is not a declared decoy is attributed by the condition it fails.

  The policy-panel mapping moved into code. The judge was frozen before the test split.
- **Reader, v2.** A genuine ambiguity is now told apart from a contestable decoy. Contestable decoys are flagged
  and kept.
- **Fixes after dev round 2**, frozen before the main runs:
  - the writer docs got Slack's primary keys and when to label a decoy F7;
  - the reader feedback lets a writer keep wording it judges clear, and asks it not to add hints;
  - the derivation prunes rows by the replica's own foreign keys. Removing a Slack user had left its memberships
    behind, so the probes did not install.

**During the main runs.** The first four changed no prompt and no test the solver saw:
- **The session limit.** It stopped 13 briefs; they were regenerated from scratch after the reset, and the first
  attempts are kept. The refused judge calls were rerun later.
- **A cached-verdict bug.** A verdict was reused across a retried attempt; verdicts are now keyed by attempt.
- **Effect locators.** They are keyed by the table's real primary key: a missing key had made the scorer fail on
  AR-SLK-23.
- **The same-day control** (amendment 3). It was added after Arm R's first scores and before any control trial.
- **Method v2** (amendment 4), a new arm with changed method notes. It was written after Arm R's autopsy and before
  any v2 scenario, and was tested on Arm P only.

**After the runs:**
- **My reading of every failing or void verdict:** 167 verdicts, 8 overridden. The one-off scripts that recorded them
  are [kit/review_control.py](kit/review_control.py), [kit/review_arm_p.py](kit/review_arm_p.py),
  [kit/review_arm_r_rest.py](kit/review_arm_r_rest.py) and [kit/review_arm_p_v2.py](kit/review_arm_p_v2.py). The Arm R
  entries were written by hand.
- **Two validity verdicts changed after I first recorded them,** on evidence from the failing verdicts, each with
  its reason in the file: AP-CAL-02 (valid to flawed; the 403) and AP2-SLK-01 (a decoy from valid to contestable).
- **Kit fixes for later use:** the reader's full-record rendering, the re-check of derived tests, and observability
  of replacement values.
- **The inputs stay frozen.** The new replica gaps are not added to `inputs/*/replica.md` (§11).

## 10. Using the kit on another domain

[kit/README.md](kit/README.md) describes the pipeline, the inputs a new domain needs, the commands, and the known
limitations. From this study, a user should plan for three manual parts:
- **The replica profile (`replica.md`).** The writer, the pre-checks and the judge all trust it. A gap missing from it
  turns into false exposures, which the judge reports as the solver's failures.
- **A validity review of the accepted scenarios.** Here it found 1 invalid scenario in all, and 2 to 8 contestable
  or invalid decoys per arm of about 60, with the reader and the checks already applied.
- **Reading the failing verdicts.** Here 8 of 167 were overridden, and the reading also found 2 decoy problems the
  review had missed.

Everything else ran unattended, once started by a prompt:
- generation, repair, checks, pre-checks and reading;
- the solver runs;
- judging and scoring.

## 11. Next steps

- **Complete the replica profiles, or fix the replica.** That means Slack's `types`, Linear's document project
  filter, and the nested Linear connections (`Issue.attachments`, `Team.cycles`, `Team.projects`,
  `Comment.children`, `projects`). Until then, a probe that depends on them measures the replica.
- **Check observability per record, through the actor's own reads.** That covers flags and permissions: it would
  have caught AR-SLK-23's group DM and AP-CAL-02's sharing rules before any run.
- **Let the judge flag a test or a decoy as invalid,** separately from the trial's outcome. That would automate the
  second validity pass and remove most of its held-out misses.
- **Target yield directly.** For example, try each probe once on a cheap solver during generation, and send decoys
  that nothing takes back to the writer. The risk is tuning the tests to one solver.
- **Compare yields over repeated generations and runs.** Two generations of the same briefs shared 7 of the 17
  facts they exposed between them. Two runs of the same exemplars shared 10 of 15 uncontested facts. One generation
  and one run per arm cannot separate a 3-fact difference from chance.
